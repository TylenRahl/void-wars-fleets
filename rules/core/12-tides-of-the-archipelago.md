# 12. Tides of the Archipelago: Battles at Sea

**Tides of the Archipelago** fights the same battles on water: the fleets become sailing warships, the
asteroid fields and planets become islands. Every rule in this book still applies, except where this
chapter changes it. These are house rules of our own; they apply only in a battle at sea.

In space ships pass over and under one another and board by touching. On the water they can't: hulls
meet, keels strike rock, and crews must cross by boat.

## The sea table

- A fleet engagement or a Cruiser Duel at sea is fought on a table **270 cm by 180 cm**, half as large
  again as the usual table, so the ships and islands have room. The other battles keep the usual table.
- The set-up zones are the same as in space.

## Islands

### The default islands

Unless you roll a battlezone, the sea has five islands, the same in every game, laid out so that neither side
has the better water:

- a big island in the middle, 28 cm across;
- two long islands, 24 cm by 14 cm, one on each side of the centre;
- two small islands, 12 cm across, in the remaining corners of the fighting area.

All of them lie between the two set-up zones, with channels at least 40 cm wide between them. This sea counts
as Deep Space, so firing sunward changes nothing.

### Rolling a battlezone at sea

You may roll a battlezone instead (chapter 10). Everything is rolled the same way, but on the sea table the
squares are **90 cm** across, and what you roll becomes land:

- an **asteroid field** or an **aether rift** becomes an island **twice** the size the field or rift would
  have been;
- a **planet** becomes a big island twice the planet's size (never more than 60 cm across), and its moons
  become islets twice a moon's size, placed as moons are;
- gas and dust clouds, rings, solar flares and radiation bursts are **not** placed at sea;
- islands keep at least 25 cm of open water between them, room for a battleship to pass.

### What an island does

- An island **blocks lines of fire**, as a planet does. There is no way to stand on it and see across.
- **Torpedoes** that strike an island are destroyed.
- A **hulk** that drifts onto an island is wrecked and destroyed.
- An island has no gravity well.

## Ships take up their whole hull

At sea a ship is drawn four times as long as in space, and it bumps into ships and islands with that whole hull,
not with its base.

- A hull is **12 cm long for each size class** of the ship: an escort 12 cm, a cruiser 24 cm, a battleship 36
  cm (heavier classes in proportion). Its width is 28% of its length. It is centred on the base and lies along
  the ship's heading.
- The hull counts **only** for running aground and for collisions. Everything else is still measured from the
  base, as in space: ranges, arcs, boarding reach and contact with ordnance.
- Defences and forts keep their bases.
- A ship can't be set up with its hull on an island or overlapping another ship's hull.

## Running aground

A ship that sails into an island **runs aground**.

1. It stops at the last point where its hull is still clear of the shore.
2. It takes **D6 hits**, with no test to avoid them. It may try to Brace for Impact! first. Its shields stop
   hits as normal, but no Blast Markers are placed.
3. It is pushed clear: it turns to face straight away from the island, and is moved until its hull is 1 cm off
   the shore.
4. Its player may then turn it once, to face any way, as long as its hull doesn't swing onto an island or
   another ship.

A ship already lying against a shore only strikes it again if it moves further into it.

## Collisions

A ship that sails into another ship **collides** with it.

- This is any other ship, friend or foe, drifting hulks included. Only ships of its own squadron are left out,
  since they sail together.
- The mover stops at the last point where its hull is clear of the other's.
- Damage goes by the two ships' sizes, ranked as for the ram test (chapter 8): escort, cruiser, battleship,
  defence. The mover always comes off worse, because it did not mean to strike.

| The moving ship is | It takes | The ship it strikes takes |
|---|---|---|
| smaller than the other | D6+2 hits | 1 hit |
| the same size | D6 hits | D3 hits |
| larger than the other | D3 hits | D6 hits |

- Shields stop the hits as normal. Both ships may Brace for Impact! first. No Blast Markers are placed.
- The mover is then pushed back the way it came until its hull is 1 cm clear, still facing the way it was going,
  and its player may turn it once to face any way (as after running aground).
- A ship already touching another only collides again if it moves in deeper.
- Torpedoes and attack craft still fly over ships and are not hurt by them.

### Ramming at sea

A deliberate ram (chapter 8) is worked out exactly as in space, and it lands when the two **hulls** meet. At sea
the rammer can't sail on through, so after the ram it stops and is pushed clear, as after a collision.

## Boarding at range

At sea a ship's crew row across in its boats, so the ships needn't touch.

- A ship may board an enemy within **15 cm**, measured base edge to base edge, that lies in its **port or
  starboard arc**. Declare it at the end of the ship's move, instead of in base contact.
- Everything else is as in chapter 8: the boarder may not fire or launch ordnance that turn, and the fight is
  rolled in the End Phase. If the enemy is out of reach by then, the boarding fails.
- A fort boards the same way, but it may reach any enemy within 15 cm of one of its sections, in any direction,
  since a fort has no port or starboard side and no ship can touch it at sea without colliding.

# 9. The End Phase and Disengaging

The End Phase settles what the turn has set in motion: boarding parties fight it out, damage crews go
to work, and the debris of battle starts to clear. This chapter also covers **disengaging**, the way a
ship slips out of a battle it can no longer win.

## The order of the End Phase

1. **Boarding actions and Hit-and-Run attacks**, teleport attacks included (chapter 8).
2. **Repairs:** both players try to repair critical damage.
3. **Blast Markers:** the player whose turn it is removes D6 of them.

## Repairs

Damage control crews work without pause: welding breaches, rerouting power, fighting fires.

- In every End Phase, each **capital ship** rolls **one D6 for each hit it has left**.
- **Each 6** repairs one critical hit or puts out one fire. Both players repair, in every End Phase.
- A ship with a **Blast Marker touching** its base rolls only **half** as many dice, rounding up.
- A Fire! critical that is not put out does 1 damage and keeps burning (chapter 7). Several unextinguished
  fires each do 1 damage.
- Bridge Smashed and Shields Collapse can never be repaired.

## Removing Blast Markers

In time the wreckage and shock waves thin out and stop troubling ships.

- The player whose turn it is rolls a D6 and removes that many Blast Markers from the table.
- Blast Markers touching a ship's base may not be removed. Apart from that, the player chooses which go.
- Blast Markers lying against an asteroid field go separately: every End Phase, each asteroid field
  loses D6 of them, on top of the D6 above (chapter 10).

## Disengaging

Sometimes a captain must run to fight another day: his ship is crippled with no hope of winning, or
hopelessly outnumbered. The old trick is a sudden change of course, then all power cut so that the
enemy loses the ship's emissions, and with luck it drifts clear and makes for home.

A ship disengages in one of two ways:

1. **Off the table.** A ship (not a hulk) that moves off a table edge, for any reason, has disengaged.
2. **By testing.** At the end of its Manoeuvre Phase, a ship or escort squadron may take a Resolve Test to
   disengage, with these modifiers:

| Situation | Modifier |
|---|---|
| a celestial phenomenon, such as an asteroid field or a planet, within 15 cm (chapter 10) | +3 |
| for every Blast Marker within 5 cm | +1 |
| for every enemy ship or ordnance marker within 15 cm | -1 |

- While a solar flare or radiation burst is raging (chapter 10), the +3 applies anywhere on the table.
- Add every modifier together before rolling. However high the total, a roll of 11 or 12 still fails.

> A Resolve 9 squadron ends its move within 5 cm of three Blast Markers and near an asteroid field, with no
> enemy within 15 cm: +4, for 13. It still needs 10 or less on the dice.

- **Passed:** remove the ship from the table. It may not come back this battle.
- **Failed:** the ship stays. For the rest of the turn it may not fire, launch ordnance or take special
  orders, Brace for Impact! aside.
- **Resolve 1 or less.** A ship or squadron whose Resolve has fallen to 1 or less, by any mix of effects,
  must try to disengage or head for the nearest table edge.

### Squadrons and disengaging

- If **any one ship** of an escort squadron disengages, the whole squadron must try to disengage at once,
  and keep trying in later turns. A squadron may not slip one or two ships away to save their points and
  throw the rest at the enemy.
- If any ship of an escort squadron moves off the table edge, the entire squadron must from then on only
  try to disengage.
- Capital ships in a squadron may disengage one by one, each testing on its **own** Resolve, not the
  squadron's best. One that tries and fails drops out of the squadron, and may not rejoin it. This is on top
  of the usual effects of failing.

### Victory points for disengaging

A disengaged ship gives the enemy a share of its points (chapter 11):

- **10%** of its points if it was not crippled when it left, or
- **25%** if it was crippled.

This is instead of, not as well as, the 25% the enemy would score for a crippled ship left on the table at
the end. An escort squadron that disengages counts as one: 10% of the value of all its escorts, or 25% if it
was crippled first, meaning that at least half its escorts (rounding up) had been destroyed.

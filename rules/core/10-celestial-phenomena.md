# 10. Celestial Phenomena

Space is not quite empty. Gas clouds, asteroid fields and planets slow ships, hide them and break up
their fire, and worlds are what fleets fight over. A good commander uses them: an ambush in a dust
cloud, a line of fire blocked by a moon, escorts kept out of sight until it is time to strike.

The battle you are playing may set some features for you. Otherwise set them up by one of the methods
below. Whatever method you use, the aim is an interesting table, not one so cluttered that the fleets
can't reach each other. A feature that will dominate the battle, such as an asteroid belt right across
the table, needs your opponent's agreement first.

## Setting up

**Method 1: one player sets the table.** One player places all the phenomena. His opponent then picks
which table edge to deploy from. (Handy when you play at one player's house: he can set up before his
opponent arrives.)

**Method 2: roll for it.** Divide the table into squares 60 cm across. Roll a D6 for each square: on a
**4 or more**, it holds celestial phenomena. Pick a battlezone (or roll a D6 for it, below), and roll
once on that battlezone's table for each square that holds something. Place each result anywhere within
its square, but not on top of another.

**Method 3: pick your ground.** Each player secretly picks a battlezone and adds its number to his fleet's
attack rating (in its fleet list). Both reveal their totals; the higher fights in the zone he picked.

## Battlezones

How close a battle is fought to the system's star decides what is found there. Roll a D6 on the table of
the zone, for each square that holds something:

| D6 | 1. Flare Region | 2. Mercurial Zone | 3. Inner Biosphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | solar flare | solar flare | a solar flare on 4-6 of a second D6, else a radiation burst |
| 2 | solar flare | radiation burst | asteroid field |
| 3 | radiation burst | asteroid field | D3 asteroid fields |
| 4 | asteroid field | D3 gas or dust clouds | D3 gas or dust clouds |
| 5 | D3 gas or dust clouds | D3 gas or dust clouds | D3 gas or dust clouds |
| 6 | planet (1-5 small, 6 medium) | planet (1-5 small, 6 medium) | planet (1-5 small, 6 medium) |

| D6 | 4. Primary Biosphere | 5. Outer Reaches | 6. Deep Space |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | asteroid field | D3+1 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields |
| 2 | D3 asteroid fields | D3 asteroid fields | asteroid field |
| 3 | gas or dust cloud | D3 gas or dust clouds | D3 gas or dust clouds |
| 4 | D3 gas or dust clouds | gas or dust cloud | gas or dust cloud |
| 5 | planet (1-5 small, 6 medium) | planet (1-3 small, 4-6 large) | aether rift |
| 6 | planet (1-5 small, 6 medium) | planet (1-3 small, 4-6 large) | small planet (a rogue world far off its orbit) |

- The Flare Region lies nearest the star, scoured by flares and burning winds; Deep Space lies far out,
  where fleets drop out of the aether.
- **Only one planet** is ever placed. If a second comes up, roll again.
- Remember to roll for a planet's moons, and for a large planet's rings (below).
- Solar flares and radiation bursts are not placed: they are tabletop effects (below).

## The sunward edge

Nearly everything in a system circles its star, so features are placed in relation to it. Before placing
anything, roll a D6 for the **sunward** edge, the table edge nearest the star:

| D6 | Sunward edge |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | the top long edge |
| 3-4 | the bottom long edge |
| 5 | the left short edge |
| 6 | the right short edge |

Bands of cloud, asteroid fields and aether rifts are laid parallel to the sunward edge.

## Tabletop features

The sizes below are guides. If your scenery is already made, use it as it is.

- A phenomenon affects a ship as soon as it touches the ship's base.
- It does not block a line of fire unless it actually lies across the line from the firing ship's stem
  to the target's stem.
- An exploding ship is not blocked by phenomena (chapter 7).

### Gas and dust clouds

Thick hydrogen or fine dust: left over from the birth of stars, the fringes of nebulae, or gas thrown off
by solar flares. Shields stop it doing harm, but ships are slowed by it, batteries can't see through it
well, and ordnance can be lost in it.

- **Effect:** a cloud counts as a **single Blast Marker in every respect**: for fire, movement, shields,
  Resolve and ordnance (chapter 5).
- A ship with 0 shields that explodes from crossing a cloud explodes at the point where it entered the
  cloud.
- Some fleets' rules let their ships test to ignore clouds.
- **Size:** bands D6×2 cm wide and D6×5 cm long, running parallel to the sunward edge.

### Asteroid fields

Rubble left from the making of the system, from a shattered world, or from an old battle.

- **Line of fire:** an asteroid field blocks it. Torpedoes that strike a field detonate.
- **Crossing:** a ship that moves through a field, or touches its edge, must pass a Resolve Test on **2D6**
  to get through safely; on **3D6** if it is on All Ahead Full. Escorts may re-roll a failed test once,
  and the second roll stands.
- **Failing:** the ship takes **D6 damage** from impacts. Its shields stop hits as normal (but no Blast
  Markers are placed for them). It is also slowed by 5 cm, as if it had crossed Blast Markers that
  turn. Brace for Impact! may be tried.
- **Squadrons** take the test once for the whole squadron. A capital ship squadron that fails: every ship
  that touches the field takes D6 damage. An escort squadron re-rolls for free; if it fails again, D6
  damage is shared out among the escorts that touched the field, in the order they moved.
- **Firing inside a field.** Ships can't fire into or out of a field. Two ships that are both inside an asteroid field may fire at each other, but only within **10 cm** with lances and batteries; such close
  fire gets no left shift for range. Sunburst cannon don't work inside a field and torpedoes of any kind
  can't be launched there. A braced or crippled ship may not fire inside a field at all.
- **Attack craft** work as normal in a field, but roll a D6 for every wave or lone marker each Ordnance
  Phase it stays in the field: on a 6 it is removed. Attack craft that fly through a field are destroyed
  on a 6.
- **Firing at a field.** To fire at an asteroid field, a ship must pass a Resolve Test, even if the field is
  its only target. Treat the field as an ordnance marker. For every 6 rolled, put a Blast Marker against
  the field on the side the fire came from. Every End Phase each field loses D6 of these, which don't
  count against the D6 Blast Markers removed as normal (chapter 9).
- **Hulks** that drift into a field are destroyed.
- **Size:** typically D3×5 cm wide and D3×5 cm long, laid parallel to the sunward edge.

### Aether rifts

Now and then a tear opens between real space and the aether: in a great aether storm, or where a large
fleet has just come through. Entering one is deadly, but a desperate captain may try it.

- A rift **blocks lines of fire**, and torpedoes that strike it detonate.
- **Attack craft** that move into a rift are destroyed. **Hulks** that drift into one vanish for ever.
- **A ship moving into a rift** must pass a Resolve Test on **3D6**.
  - **Passed:** it comes out on the other side. Roll 2D6×10 cm: the ship is carried that far on along its
    heading.
  - **Failed:** the ship is gone from the battle, lost in the aether.
- **Size:** D3×5 cm wide and D3×10 cm long.

### Planets

Most planets are airless rock or wrapped in poison air, but worlds are what systems are fought for, so
battles often rage close to them.

- A planet is a round template (or a model). It **blocks lines of fire**, unless a ship's stem is on the
  template: then the template doesn't block that ship's line of sight, or anyone's line of sight to it.
  Ships on the same template can all see each other.
- A ship is *on* the template only if its **stem** touches it, not merely its base.
- Ships may move "through" a planet, passing over or under it.
- **Torpedoes** are destroyed when they touch the template's edge, whether fired at the planet or fired
  outward by ships on it. Ships on a template may launch torpedoes, which are removed as they reach its edge.
- **Hulks** that drift into a planet are destroyed.

| Planet | Template | Gravity well, from the template's edge |
|---|---|---|
| small (like the smallest rocky worlds) | up to 15 cm across | up to 10 cm |
| medium (like a world of air and oceans) | 16-25 cm across | up to 15 cm |
| large (a gas giant) | 26-50 cm across | up to 30 cm |

The game uses templates of 12, 20 and 40 cm, and moons of 4 cm.

#### Gravity wells

Every planet's pull reaches out around it: its **gravity well**.

- A ship whose stem is in a gravity well may make a **free turn of up to 45°** at the start of its move and
  another at the end. Each free turn must be towards the planet: towards the centre of the planet (or moon)
  or by 45°, whichever is less.
- The free turns need no minimum move first, and can be made even when the ship couldn't otherwise turn,
  on All Ahead Full or Lock On. They can be added to Come to New Heading.
- They can only be made at the start and at the end of the move, and only while the ship is in the well.
- **High orbit.** A ship in a gravity well may choose to stay in high orbit. It need not move at all, but a
  ship that does stand still is fired at on the Defences column.
- **Low orbit.** A ship in a gravity well may enter low orbit in some battles (below).

#### Moons

Larger moons, thousands of kilometres across, count as small planets in every way, their gravity wells
included. A medium planet has D3-1 moons, a large one D6-2. Moons are up to 5 cm across. Place each 2D6×10
cm from the planet, in a random direction.

#### Rings

A large planet has rings if you roll **5+** on a D6. Place D3 rings around it, and roll a D6
for each: 1-4 gas and dust, 5-6 asteroids. Each ring is D6 cm wide and starts D6×5 cm out from the planet's
edge. Rings may run into one another.

## Tabletop effects

These affect the whole table, and can be combined with features: a fight round a planet close to a sun, for
example. "The start of each turn" here means the start of each game turn.

### Firing sunward

Near a star its glare hides ships at long range, while close up a ship with the star behind it stands out
sharply. This only counts in the Flare Region, the Mercurial Zone and the Inner Biosphere. Further out the
star is too distant to matter.

- Put the bearing compass on the firing ship with its arrows parallel to the sunward edge. A target in the arc
  that faces the sunward edge is being fired at **sunward**.
- When firing sunward, the range shifts on the gunnery table are doubled: beyond 30 cm, **two** columns right;
  within 15 cm, **two** columns left.

### Solar flares

Now and then a star throws out a burst of energy hundreds of millions of kilometres across. It floods space
with charged particles and magnetic shock: shields are nearly swamped, and a ship without them is hurt.

- At the start of each turn, roll a D6 for each solar flare the battlezone produced. On a **6**, a flare
  erupts. A flare only comes **once** a game; after it, stop rolling.
- Every ship on the table gets one Blast Marker, placed on its sunward side.
- Every ship **without shields** takes 1 hit; roll a D6 for it, and on a 4+ that hit is also a critical hit.
- Every ordnance marker is swept away on a D6 roll of 4+.
- Some fleets' rules let their ships test to escape a flare.

### Radiation bursts

A star also throws out bursts of radiation and radio noise that scramble signals between ships and even
inside them. Commanding a ship through one is very hard.

- At the start of each turn, roll a D6 for each radiation burst the battlezone produced. On a **5 or 6** a
  burst occurs. Only one burst can happen in a turn, however many you rolled for.
- Roll a D6 for its strength. Every ship on the table has its Resolve lowered by that much for the rest of the
  turn. A 3 means -3 Resolve for every ship.
- While the burst lasts, a fleet commander's re-rolls can only be used for Command checks of his own ship or
  squadron.

## Fighting in low orbit

Some battles let ships drop into low orbit to attack a world: bombarding it, landing troops, or worse. Low
orbit is fought on a small table of its own (45-60 cm wide by 90-135 cm long), or on paper or markers at a
scale of 1 mm to 1 cm, beside the main table. One long edge of it is the planet itself.

- **Going in.** A ship in a planet's gravity well may drop into low orbit when one of its turns begins. Place it
  on the low orbit table, touching the edge furthest from the planet.
- **Moving.** Ships in low orbit need not move, and capital ships need no distance before turning.
- **The pull.** At the start of **each player's** turn, every ship within 45 cm of the planet edge is moved
  straight towards the planet, without turning: **5 cm** for a small planet, **8 cm** for a medium one, **10 cm**
  for a large one.
  - A ship pulled off the planet edge is destroyed.
  - An escort or transport that chooses to move off the planet edge has landed, and is removed. Capital ships
    can't land.
- **Firing.** The planet's gravity and the top of its air spoil every shot: batteries shift one column right,
  lances and sunburst cannon must roll a 4+ before they can fire at all, and ships can't fire torpedoes.
- **Leaving.** A ship that moves off any other edge of the low orbit table has climbed out of low orbit. It goes back on
  the main table, against the planet's edge. A ship can't enter low orbit and leave it in the same turn.
- Otherwise ships move and fight in low orbit as normal, in each player's turn.

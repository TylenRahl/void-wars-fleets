# 8. Ramming, Boarding and Hit-and-Run

Most battles are fought with guns at long range. This chapter covers the moments when ships come to
grips: driving one hull into another, sending crews across to storm an enemy, and small raids that
slip troops aboard to wreck one vital system.

## Ramming

The table is flat but space is not, so ships pass over and under each other freely. A ship only
strikes another when its captain sets out to do it.

### Declaring a ram

1. Only a ship on **All Ahead Full** may ram, and only one target per Manoeuvre Phase, however many
   enemy ships lie in its path.
2. Once its All Ahead Full Command check has passed, **declare the target**, and take the Resolve Test
   to ram, *before* rolling its extra 4D6 cm. The choice can't be changed afterwards, whatever else the
   ship ends up crossing or touching, and even if it never reaches its target.
3. The test depends on the two ships' sizes. From largest to smallest: **defence, battleship, cruiser,
   escort**.

| The target is | Resolve Test to ram |
|---|---|
| smaller than the rammer | 3D6 |
| the same size | 2D6 |
| larger than the rammer | 1D6 |

- No separate test is needed to pass by nearer enemies in order to ram one further off.
- **Failed:** the captain misses his chance. The ship simply moves on All Ahead Full.
- **Passed:** the ship must move its **full** distance, the extra All Ahead Full distance included.

> A cruiser rams another cruiser on 2D6, an escort on 3D6 and a battleship on 1D6. It's far easier for
> an escort to ram a battleship than the other way round.

### The ram

The ram lands if any part of the rammer's base touches any part of the target's base during its move.
Stop the move there and work out the ram:

1. **The rammer strikes.** Roll one D6 for each of the rammer's **starting** hits (not the hits it has
   left). Each dice equal to or over the target's Armour, on the side struck, is 1 damage.
2. **The target strikes back.** It rolls one D6 for each of **half** its starting hits (rounding up),
   against the rammer's **front** Armour. Each success is 1 damage.
   - If the two met **head on**, prow to prow, the target rolls one dice for each of its **full**
     starting hits instead.
   - A **defence** always strikes back with its full starting hits, as if prow on: defences are built
     more solidly than ships.
3. **Shields don't stop ram damage.** Both ships may Brace for Impact! as normal.
4. Then the rammer finishes its move.

> A cruiser with 8 starting hits rams an enemy cruiser in the starboard side. It rolls 8 dice against
> Armour 5 and scores 3. The target, also with 8 starting hits, rolls 4 dice against the rammer's
> armoured prow, 6+, and scores 1.

- **A ship destroyed by a ram** explodes at once, if it explodes, and both ships are always caught in
  the blast. If the target is the one that blew up, finish the rammer's move as normal; it will usually
  lose speed to the explosion's Blast Markers.
- **Ram, then board.** A ship that has rammed may still fire, or board later, as normal for a ship on
  All Ahead Full. It may only board a ship it is touching at the end of its full move, which need not be
  the ship it rammed.

## Boarding actions

In a boarding action a ship comes alongside an enemy and sends its crew across by teleporter, shuttle,
pod or simply in void suits, to seize a foothold, blow holes in the hull and fight through the
corridors. Both sides can lose: even the winner may be badly hurt, and the loser may explode.

### Declaring a boarding

- A ship may board an enemy ship whose base it is **touching**.
- Declare it in the **Manoeuvre Phase**, at the moment the ships make contact.
- You may only start a boarding action in your own turn. You may defend against one in the enemy's turn.
- A ship that boards may **not fire or launch ordnance** in the same turn: its crew are busy.
- The boarding is fought in the **End Phase**, before any repairs are tried and before Blast Markers are
  removed.

### Boarding value

- A ship's **boarding value** is the hits it has left, changed by any rule of its fleet or upgrades (some
  double it, some halve it).
- The ship being boarded then adds its **turrets** left.

### Fighting it out

Both players roll a D6 and add their modifiers. The total is not limited to 6.

| Situation | Modifier |
|---|---|
| your boarding value is higher than the enemy's\* | +1 |
| your boarding value is twice the enemy's\* | +2 |
| your boarding value is three times the enemy's\* | +3 |
| your boarding value is four or more times the enemy's\* | +4 |
| the enemy ship has a Blast Marker touching its base | +1 |
| the enemy ship is crippled | +2 |
| the enemy ship is on special orders | +1 |
| your ship is Rukka or Dominion | +1 |
| your ship is Concord | +2 |

\* Use only the highest of these four.

The higher total wins. **The loser takes 1 damage for each point it lost by.** The damage is not checked
for critical hits as damage usually is; instead, both ships may suffer a critical hit, as the table says:

| Lost by | Result | The winner scores a critical on the loser on | The loser scores a critical on the winner on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stalemate | 5+ | 5+ |
| 2 | Heavy Fighting | 4+ | 5+ |
| 3 | Driven Back | 3+ | 6+ |
| 4 | Stormed | 2+ | 6+ |
| 5 or more | Overwhelmed | automatic | none |

Roll a D6 for each ship that might suffer one; on the score shown, roll on the critical hits table
(chapter 7) for it. Brace for Impact! gives no save against anything in a boarding action.

> A ship with 6 hits left boards a ship with 3 left. After modifiers the attacker scores 6, the defender
> 4. The defender lost by 2, so it takes 2 damage. Heavy Fighting: it suffers a critical on a 4+, and the
> attacker suffers one on a 5+.

- **A draw.** If the totals are equal, the ships are locked together and fight again in every End Phase.
  Neither may move, fire or launch ordnance until one of them has been reduced to 0 hits. The survivor
  may move off and fight on in its next turn.
- **Reduced to 0.** A ship reduced to 0 hits in a boarding action does not roll on the catastrophic
  damage table: it becomes a **drifting hulk** at once. If the last hit came from a critical, roll for
  catastrophic damage as normal.
- Ships that survive being boarded may move away as normal in their next Manoeuvre Phase.

### Several ships boarding one

When several ships board one enemy, their player may send them in together or one at a time.

- **Together:** add their boarding values and make one roll. Race modifiers count once only, and only the
  best: two ships of one fleet with +1 and +2 crews get +2, not +3. The attacking player shares out any
  damage among his ships, but **each** boarding ship risks its own critical hit.
- **Separately:** each capital ship, or each escort squadron, fights its own action, and the defender rolls
  against each one. Its damage adds up. Each attacker uses only its own boarding value, not the values of
  the others.

### Escorts boarding

Escorts may board using these rules, capital ships included, though they only stand a chance against a
badly damaged one. Remember that a critical hit destroys an escort, so an escort can win a boarding
action and still be destroyed by it.

## Hit-and-Run attacks

A **Hit-and-Run attack** sends a small band of picked troops aboard an enemy ship to blow up one part of
it. They arrive by assault boat (chapter 6), by boarding torpedo (chapter 6), or by teleporter from a
nearby ship. A ship left crippled by such raids can be finished with gunnery later.

### Resolving it

Roll a D6:

- **1:** the attack fails.
- **2 to 6:** look the score up on the critical hits table (chapter 7) and apply that result at once: 2
  is Dorsal Armament Damaged, through to 6, Engine Room Damaged.
- **Against an escort:** instead, the escort is destroyed on a 4+.

Some fleets add +1 to their Hit-and-Run rolls, so they roll 2 to 7 and always do some harm; against
escorts they need a 3+. Some fleets give -1 to enemy Hit-and-Run attacks against them: against their
escorts the attacker needs a 5+.

- The critical damage of a Hit-and-Run attack can be repaired as normal.
- A ship on Brace for Impact! saves against it (chapter 4). An escort can brace against one too.
- A ship whose rules say it can't be boarded is **not** protected from Hit-and-Run attacks.

### Teleport attacks

A ship close to an enemy may teleport a raiding party across.

- **Who:** capital ships on Lock On, Reload Ordnance or no special order. Any other order rules it out.
  A crippled ship may not teleport. Escorts and defences with only 1 hit left may not either.
- **When:** in the End Phase.
- **The target:** an enemy ship within **10 cm** whose **shields are down** (active shields scatter the
  beam), and which has no more hits left than the attacker.
- **How many:** one teleport attack per ship each turn. Each is one Hit-and-Run attack against the
  target.

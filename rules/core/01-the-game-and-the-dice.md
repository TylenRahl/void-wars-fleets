# 1. The Game and the Dice

VOID WARS: Fleets is a game of starship battles for two players. Each of you commands a fleet:
great capital ships, and the swarms of small escorts that run beside them. You take turns to steer
your ships, fire their guns, launch torpedoes and attack craft, and send boarding crews across.
When the battle ends, you count up the damage done to work out who has won.

This chapter covers what every later chapter takes for granted: what you need, how distance is
measured, where a ship actually is, the dice, and the four arcs around every ship.

## What you need

- Two players, and a model on a flying base for every ship in each fleet.
- A table to fight over. The game uses a table 180 cm long and 120 cm wide.
- A tape measure or ruler marked in centimetres. **Every distance in this book is in centimetres
  (cm).**
- A handful of six-sided dice, a pen, and a roster to note damage, orders and Resolve.
- Markers for Blast Markers, torpedo salvoes and attack craft (described where they first appear).
- A round template for the sunburst cannon, and a scatter dice (chapter 5).

## Measuring

- You may measure any distance at any time, for moving or for range, unless both players agree
  to play without it.
- **A ship is a point.** In space a ship's model is far too large for the table, so the game
  treats the ship as standing exactly where the **stem** of its flying base is.
  - A move is measured from the stem.
  - The range of a weapon is measured from the firing ship's stem to the target's stem.
- **The base is the ship's close surroundings.** The base stands for the space just around the
  ship, a few thousand kilometres at most. Anything that touches the base touches the ship: a
  torpedo salvo, a squadron of bombers, an asteroid field, a Blast Marker. A ship is also affected
  by a hazard as soon as its base moves into contact with it.
- **The table is flat; space is not.** Ships are free to pass over and under one another, so two
  ships never collide unless one of them sets out to ram (chapter 8). They may finish a move
  overlapping. Do this only when the move or a firing position truly needs it; it is not a way
  to bunch up for its own sake. When models don't fit, lift them off their stems or mark their
  places with an empty base.

## Dice

### The D6

Every roll uses ordinary six-sided dice, called **D6**.

- **D6:** roll one dice.
- **2D6, 3D6 and so on:** roll that many and add the scores. 2D6 runs from 2 to 12, 3D6 from 3
  to 18.
- **D6+2, D6-2 and so on:** roll, then add or take away the number. D6+2 gives 3 to 8.
- **D6×5 and so on:** roll and multiply. D6×5 gives 5 to 30.
- Mixed forms work the same way: 2D6+5 gives 7 to 17, and 3D6-3 gives 0 to 15.
- **D3:** roll a D6, halve it and round up. 1-2 is a 1, 3-4 is a 2, 5-6 is a 3.
- **4+, 5+ and so on:** the score you need on one dice. A roll of 4+ succeeds on a 4, 5 or 6.

### The scatter dice

The **scatter dice** has arrows on four of its faces and a hit symbol on the other two. It gives a
random direction (the way the arrow points) or, on a hit, no movement at all. It is used for the
sunburst cannon (chapter 5).

### Re-rolls

Some rules let you roll a dice again. Pick up the dice you are allowed to re-roll and roll them.

- The second roll stands, even if it is worse than the first.
- A Command check or any other Resolve Test takes one re-roll at most, wherever the re-roll
  comes from. If a re-roll fails, no further re-roll can be spent on that test, even if you have
  more to spare.

### Rolling off

When a rule tells both players to roll off, each rolls a D6. The higher score wins; roll again on
a tie.

## Arcs and discipline

Around every ship lie four **arcs**, each a quarter of the circle (90 degrees), centred on the stem:

- the **front** arc, straight ahead,
- the **rear** arc, straight behind,
- the **left** (port) arc and the **right** (starboard) arc, to either side.

A bearing compass, a round card marked with the four quarters, makes the arcs easy to see: put its
centre over the stem with its arrows along the length of the ship.

Arcs are used two ways:

1. **Fire arcs.** A weapon can only fire at a target in the arc, or arcs, it is mounted to cover
   (chapter 5).
2. **Discipline.** Put the compass over the *target* instead. The arc of the target that the firing
   ship sits in is the discipline the target shows to it:
   - the firer is in the target's front arc: the target is **closing**,
   - in its rear arc: the target is **moving away**,
   - in its left or right arc: the target is **abeam**.

If a line of fire runs exactly along the line between two arcs, the player who is firing chooses
which arc it falls in. This holds whether the arc in question is the firer's or the target's.

## Game turns and player turns

A **game turn** is one turn for each player. A battle that lasts eight game turns gives each of you
eight turns of your own, sixteen in all. Chapter 3 covers what happens inside a turn.

## Fleet lists

Your fleet list is normally open: your opponent may read it at any time. If you both agree, you may
hide your lists from each other until the battle is over, but each must be written down in full, with every
upgrade and points total, before play begins. If either player asks to see the other's list, both
lists are shown at once.

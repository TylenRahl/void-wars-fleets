# 11. Fleets, Squadrons and Commanders

This chapter covers choosing a fleet, grouping its ships into squadrons, the commander who leads it,
setting up a straight fleet battle, and counting victory points.

## Choosing a fleet

- Agree a points value with your opponent. Each of you picks a fleet up to that value from your
  fleet's list. A ship's points include any upgrades bought for it, and the points of a commander on
  board.
- Each fleet's own list says which ships it may take, how many of each, which upgrades are allowed and
  who leads it. Those rules are in the fleet's own chapter.
- **Escorts** are taken in squadrons of 2 to 6, unless the fleet list says otherwise. A single escort may
  stand alone only if it is the fleet's only escort.
- **Capital ship squadrons** are declared before the battle:
  - cruisers, heavy cruisers and battlecruisers go 2 to 4 to a squadron, mixed as you like;
  - battleships and grand cruisers go 2 or 3 to a squadron, and are not mixed with the smaller classes;
  - escorts, transports and derelicts never join a capital ship squadron.

## Squadrons

Small ships can do little alone, so escorts fight in squadrons, moving together and massing their guns.
Battleships and cruisers usually fight alone, but in a big battle they too may be grouped. Whatever a fleet
calls them (flotillas, packs, battle groups), squadrons all work the same way.

### Escort squadrons

- An escort squadron may hold up to six ships, unless its fleet list says otherwise.
- Its ships may be of different classes, as long as they are all escorts. (Ships of one class are simpler to
  move and give orders to, but it's up to you.)
- It has **one** Resolve for the whole squadron, rolled once (chapter 2).

### Orders for a squadron

- A squadron takes **one** Command check for all its ships. A capital ship squadron uses the **highest**
  Resolve left among its ships; each ship keeps its own Resolve for everything else.
- The usual modifiers apply, even if only some of its ships have Blast Markers touching them.
- **Every ship** in the squadron is on the order, whichever ship it was taken for. All Ahead Full rolls its
  extra distance once for the whole squadron.
- A squadron, like a ship, can be on only one special order in a turn.
- **Brace for Impact!** braces every ship in the squadron, capital ship squadrons included, not only the ship
  under fire. It replaces any order the squadron was on.

### Formation

- Every ship must stay within **15 cm** of at least one other ship of the squadron, so that the squadron forms
  one unbroken chain.
- Ships of a squadron move one at a time, as they like, within the normal rules, as long as they finish in
  formation.
- A ship that drops out of formation (badly handled, or left behind when the ships around it are destroyed)
  no longer counts as part of the squadron until it is back within 15 cm of another of its ships. It must
  rejoin as soon as it can, and until then may not use the squadron's special orders.

### Firing at a squadron

The ships of a squadron often show different disciplines to a firer: some closing, some abeam.

- **Pick a category.** The firing player chooses which category of target he shoots at (closing, abeam and so
  on). His hits can't touch ships that are harder to hit than that. A squadron with two ships closing and two
  abeam, shot at as if all were closing, can only be hit on the two closing ships.
- **Hits go to the nearest.** Each hit goes to the **nearest eligible ship** of the squadron until it is
  destroyed, then to the next nearest, and so on.
- **Only ships under fire.** Hits can only be given to ships actually in range and in arc. However many hits
  the squadron takes, they are shared only among those ships. An escort squadron taking ten hits from
  gunnery with only three escorts in range and arc loses three escorts at most.
- **No picking.** A Resolve Test can't be used to pick out one ship in a squadron. The only ways to reach a
  further ship with gunnery are a column further to the left on the gunnery table for it, or weaker armour
  that only the hits that can't hurt the nearest ship can damage. Weapons that ignore armour can't use the
  second.
- These rules are for shooting only. Ordnance, sunburst cannon and boarding actions affect only the ships
  they actually touch. Hits from an asteroid field on an escort squadron, though, go to every escort that
  touched it.
- **Mixed armour.** If the ships have different armour (or different armour on the sides facing the firer),
  roll against the **lowest** armour among them. Then give the dice out one at a time, lowest first, each to
  the nearest ship it can damage.

> A battery fires at a squadron of a frigate with Armour 4+ and a destroyer with Armour 5+ and scores 4, 5
> and 6. If the frigate is nearer, all three can hurt it and they go to it first. If the destroyer is nearer,
> the 4 goes to the frigate and the 5 and 6 to the destroyer.

- **Capital ship squadrons.** When hits are given out one at a time, roll for every critical hit on each ship
  as you go, and apply its extra damage, before the ship counts as destroyed. Only then do further hits pass
  to the next ship. This matters most for ships that take extra damage instead of rolling criticals.
- If a whole capital ship squadron disengages, each ship counts its own victory points, not as an escort
  squadron does.

### A squadron firing

A squadron fires as one ship would, with the same rules.

- To fire at anything but its nearest enemy it must pass a Resolve Test. Ships out of range or arc of the
  squadron's chosen target may fire at another enemy.
- **Batteries** of a squadron add their firepower together before going to the gunnery table. Where ships
  fire from different disciplines or ranges, work out each group's dice separately and add them before rolling.
  Five frigates firing at a cruiser, two behind it and three abeam: the two work out their dice together as
  moving away, the three as abeam, and the two totals are added.
- **Halving.** An escort squadron on an order that halves its guns adds up its firepower and halves the
  total (rounding up). Capital ships are halved ship by ship (rounding up), even in a squadron. An escort
  squadron is not halved just because it is crippled (half its ships lost).
- **Ordnance.** Ships of a squadron launch ordnance one by one, unless they are in base contact with one
  another. Ships of one squadron touching each other may join their ordnance into one bigger wave or salvo.
  A joined salvo must lie in the fire arc of every ship firing it, and is measured from the stem of the ship
  furthest from where the salvo ends its move, so the group gets no extra speed. Some fleets may never join
  salvoes.

## Fleet commanders

Most fleets are led by a commander, whose name and cost are in the fleet list.

- He goes aboard one of your capital ships, chosen before the battle: his **flagship**.
- His Resolve **replaces** the ship's own, even if it is lower.
- He brings one or more **re-rolls** (some lists let you buy more). Spend one on a failed Command check or
  Resolve Test; the second roll stands. Only one re-roll can be used on any one test, from whatever source.
- If his flagship suffers **Bridge Smashed**, the fleet loses all the commander re-rolls it has left.
- During a radiation burst his re-rolls can only be spent on Command checks for his own ship or squadron
  (chapter 10).
- His points, and anything bought for him, count as part of his ship's value for victory points.

## Before the battle

1. **Pick the battle** you will fight (a fleet engagement, below, unless you agree on another).
2. **Decide who attacks**, if the battle has an attacker and a defender: agree, roll for it, or each roll
   dice equal to your fleet's attack rating and the highest single dice attacks.
3. **Pick your fleets** as the battle says.
4. **Set up celestial phenomena** (chapter 10).
5. **Roll Resolve** for your ships and squadrons (chapter 2).
6. **Declare capital ship squadrons**, and put your commanders on their ships.
7. **Deploy** as the battle says.
8. **Find who goes first** as the battle says.
9. Fight.

## A fleet engagement

Small actions are fought for convoys or raids. But sometimes two whole fleets come to battle: to hold a system, to break through, or for nothing more than the other's ruin.

- **Forces:** both fleets are picked to the same points value.
- **Battlezone:** usually the primary or inner biosphere, keeping a world out of bombardment range; it can be
  anywhere. Set up celestial phenomena in any way both players accept.
- **Set-up:** each fleet sets up in a zone **30 cm deep along one long table edge**, the two zones facing each
  other across the table. Roll a D6 to see which fleet takes which zone.
  - Both players roll a D6. The **lower** scorer places one squadron or lone ship first. Then the players take
    turns, one squadron or lone ship at a time, until every ship is down. A squadron is placed whole before
    the other player places again.
  - Ships are placed facing the far long edge.
- **First turn:** once all are placed, both players roll a D6; the higher chooses whether to go first or second.
- **Length:** until one fleet has disengaged or been destroyed.
- **Victory:** both fleets score victory points as normal. The higher total wins.

## Victory points

Victory points measure how much harm each fleet has done to the other. A battle may add more for its own
objectives.

| For each enemy | Victory points |
|---|---|
| ship destroyed | its full points value |
| capital ship crippled (and still on the table at the end) | 25% of its points value |
| ship or escort squadron disengaged, crippled before it left | 25% of its points value |
| ship or escort squadron disengaged, not crippled | 10% of its points value |
| hulk on the table, if you hold the field | 50% of its points value |

- Round every percentage up.
- A ship's points value includes its upgrades, and the commander on board with whatever was bought for him.
- **Escorts** score their points only when their **whole** squadron has been destroyed. An escort squadron
  counts as crippled once it has lost half its ships, rounding up.
- **Holding the field.** If at the end every enemy ship has been destroyed or has disengaged, and you have at
  least one working ship of your own on the table, you hold the field. You then score half the points value of
  **every** hulk on the table, your own as well as the enemy's: your own wrecks, at least, are kept out of
  enemy hands.
- Planetary defences destroyed score their points value, where a battle uses them.

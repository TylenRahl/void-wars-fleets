# 6. Ordnance

**Ordnance** covers everything a ship launches rather than fires: torpedoes the size of towers, and
swarms of small attack craft (fighters, bombers and assault boats). Ordnance is launched in the
Fire Phase, moves in the Ordnance Phase, and is shown on the table by markers.

## Launching and reloading

- **Launching.** Declare and place ordnance at the **end of the Fire Phase**. Put the marker on the
  launching ship's base to show it has gone. It moves and attacks in the Ordnance Phase.
- **Ships start loaded.** Every ship begins the battle with its torpedoes in the tubes and its attack
  craft fuelled.
- **One launch empties it.** Launching any amount of ordnance uses that ordnance up. The ship must go
  on **Reload Ordnance** (chapter 4) before it can launch that kind again.
  - A carrier with four bays that is only allowed to launch two squadrons cannot keep the other two
    back for later: it must reload before launching more.
  - Torpedoes and attack craft are separate. A ship that carries both and launches only one may still
    launch the other later without reloading. If it launched its attack craft last turn and then
    failed to reload, it may still fire its torpedoes.
- **Loaded stays loaded.** A ship that has never launched, or has reloaded, stays loaded for as many
  turns as it doesn't launch, whatever orders it takes in between.
- Crippling and Brace for Impact! halve torpedo tubes and launch bays as they halve guns (chapters 4
  and 7).

### The fleet's limit on attack craft

- A fleet may never have more attack craft markers on the table than it has **working launch bays**
  in total. This is a limit for the whole fleet, not for each ship. Any carrier that is loaded may
  launch, even a partial wave, as long as the fleet's total stays within the limit.
- Count only the bays the fleet still has: a crippled carrier's bays are halved, and a destroyed
  carrier's bays are lost.
- A fleet whose rules say it cannot run out of ordnance may have up to **twice** its bays in play,
  and such a ship launches twice its bays.
- If more attack craft are already in play than the fleet's bays allow, it may not launch any.
- A loaded ship may **recall** craft already in play: remove them at once, and launch new ones from
  its stem. Recalled craft are simply removed; they cannot attack anything, enemy ordnance included,
  on their way out. (So a carrier cannot strike with one wave and launch a fresh one in the same
  turn.) Torpedoes have no such limit.

## Moving ordnance

- In each Ordnance Phase **both** players move the ordnance they have in play, including ordnance
  launched in earlier turns. The player whose turn it is moves all of theirs first. In the game,
  torpedoes of both sides move by themselves when the phase opens (the side whose turn it is first);
  attack craft are then moved by hand.
- Every kind of ordnance has a speed: the distance it moves each Ordnance Phase.
- The markers of one salvo or wave are kept touching one another. They are never stacked.

### Contact

- When an ordnance marker touches a ship's base or another ordnance marker, the result is worked out
  **at once**. This includes the Manoeuvre Phase, when a ship moves into enemy ordnance.
- Ordnance must deal with the **first** ordnance or ship it touches. It does not have to go for the
  nearest target, but it cannot pass by what it meets. A squadron of fighters touching a small
  salvo can't skip it for a bigger one beyond; a wave touching an escort can't skip it for a nearby
  cruiser. (So a small salvo can be sent ahead to clear enemy fighters out of the way of a big
  one.)
- **Ordnance ignores shields.**
- If two ships with the same size of base sit in exactly the same place, so that no one can tell which
  is nearer to the incoming ordnance, the attacking player picks which one is attacked.

### Ordnance and Blast Markers

Ordnance has no shields. If a marker moves through any Blast Markers, roll a D6: on a 6 it is
removed.

- Roll once per move, however many markers it crosses. A whole salvo or wave rolls once.
- Ordnance attacking a ship that has a Blast Marker touching it must also take this roll, because
  the ship's whole base is covered by the marker.

### Firing at ordnance

Ships may fire at ordnance in the Fire Phase with direct fire weapons.

- No Resolve Test is needed to ignore a nearer enemy ship and fire at ordnance, nor to ignore
  ordnance that happens to be the nearest target.
- A test **is** needed to split fire between ordnance targets, as for splitting fire normally, and to
  fire at a marker, wave or salvo that is not the nearest enemy ordnance in range.
- Weapons batteries use the **ordnance** column of the gunnery table. Discipline does not count; the range
  and Blast Marker shifts still apply.
- Batteries and lances both need **6s** to hit ordnance.
- A marker that is hit is removed. So is any ordnance caught by a sunburst cannon's template.

## Torpedoes

A torpedo is a long-range missile with a plasma drive, which also forms most of its warhead. A
torpedo salvo has a **Strength** (how many torpedoes) and a **speed**, both on the launching ship's
profile. Torpedo tubes are usually in the prow.

- **The marker.** One marker shows the whole salvo, with a dice beside it for its Strength. It is no
  more than 2.5 cm wide.
- **Launching.** At the end of its first move, put the salvo where it is **wholly inside the launching
  ship's fire arc**. Then trace its path back, making any attacks along the way. It can never attack
  anything outside its own fire arc, however close the target is to the launching ship.
- **Moving.** Torpedoes fly straight on, a full move at their speed every Ordnance Phase, until they
  strike something or leave the table. They cannot slow down. The centre of the salvo stays on its
  line of fire.
- **No free passes.** Torpedoes hit whatever they meet, friendly ships included. A ship touching
  another friendly ship may launch through that ship's base, whether or not they are in a
  squadron. Ships not in one squadron may not join their salvoes (chapter 11).
- **Hulks** are not ignored either: a torpedo that meets one strikes it.

### A torpedo hit

When the salvo touches a ship's base:

1. The ship's turrets fire first (below).
2. Roll a D6 for each torpedo left in the salvo. Each roll equal to or over the ship's **Armour** does
   1 damage. In the game the armour used is the one on the side the torpedoes come from. Shields are
   ignored.
3. The salvo loses 1 Strength for each hit it scored, and flies on. It may hit other ships further
   along its path.

> A salvo of Strength 6 hits a ship: six dice, three hits. It flies on at Strength 3 for the rest of
> its move, and will strike anything else in its way.

- **Once per turn.** No salvo may attack the same ship twice in one game turn, even if it starts the
  next phase still touching it. The torpedoes are taken to have passed on beyond it. A salvo still
  attacks any other ship that comes into contact with it.

### Premature detonation

A whole salvo is removed if:

- it moves through any Blast Markers and rolls a 6,
- direct fire scores any hit on it, or
- it touches an enemy torpedo or fighter marker.

### Splitting a salvo

- A ship that launches six torpedoes or fewer may not split them.
- A ship that launches more than six may split them into two salvoes, each with its own marker. The
  two must go in different directions (never both at the same target or along the same line), and
  neither may have fewer than three torpedoes.
- A squadron need not fire all its torpedoes as one salvo.

### Turning torpedoes

Torpedoes that are allowed to turn do so from the centre of the marker at the start of the Ordnance
Phase, never in the phase they were launched (so they are always launched into the arc ordinary
torpedoes would use). When a salvo turns, no edge of it may move further than its speed; the inside
edge may move less.

### Boarding torpedoes

Boarding torpedoes carry assault troops who punch through the hull and attack from inside.

- They are launched like ordinary torpedoes. The fleet lists say which ships carry them.
- A ship cannot launch ordinary and boarding torpedoes in the same turn.
- At the start of every Ordnance Phase after the one they were launched in, a boarding salvo may turn
  up to 45°, from the centre of its marker.
- They attack as ordinary torpedoes (turrets, then hit rolls), but **each hit makes a Hit-and-Run
  attack** (chapter 8) instead of doing damage. These attacks are made in the Ordnance Phase.
- They are removed by any other torpedoes they touch, except friendly boarding torpedoes.
- They do not attack friendly ships (hulks included), friendly fighters don't remove them, and they
  may pass by hulks if their player chooses.

## Turrets

A ship's main guns are far too big and slow to track ordnance in its final run. Its turrets fire the
moment ordnance touches its base.

| Against | Each turret dice of 4, 5 or 6 |
|---|---|
| a torpedo salvo | takes 1 torpedo off the salvo's Strength |
| attack craft | destroys one squadron (one marker) |

- **Torpedoes or attack craft.** In one Ordnance Phase a ship's turrets may fire at every torpedo
  salvo that attacks it, *or* at every wave of attack craft, but not at both. So a ship
  can be swamped by both kinds at once. In the game the choice lasts for the player's whole turn,
  since ships also meet ordnance while moving.
- Turrets don't fire at fighters that reach the ship: fighters can't harm a ship (below).

### Massing turrets

Ships whose bases touch may cover one another.

- Each ship touching the ship under attack adds **+1 turret dice**, to a maximum of **+3**, however
  many ships are touching.
- Ships that mass turrets with the target are bound by the same choice it made, torpedoes or attack
  craft, for that phase.
- Only the target's own turrets come off a bomber's attacks; the dice added by other ships do not.
- A braced ship may mass turrets and may be helped. A crippled ship may not add its turrets, but may
  be helped.
- A ship with no turrets (a hulk, for example) adds nothing, but may be helped by a ship with turrets.
- As only one ship moves at a time, ships only mass turrets before or after the Manoeuvre Phase, not
  during it. The single exception: a moving ship that touches an enemy marker at the same moment that
  a friendly ship comes into contact with it may mass with that friend.

## Attack craft

Attack craft are launched from **launch bays**. A bay launches one squadron, so a carrier with four
bays launches four squadrons at once. Each squadron is one **20 mm square marker**. When it launches,
the carrier chooses which kinds go: fighters, bombers, assault boats, or a mix, as far as the fleet
list allows. Each kind has its own marker.

- **Moving.** Attack craft turn freely and move in any direction, up to their speed (on the ship's
  profile).
- **What they meet.** They are able to avoid targets and obstacles they don't want, unless their path
  can't help touching them: Blast Markers, other ordnance, celestial phenomena. Whatever they do
  touch, they must deal with as their rules below say. Fighters must deal with any torpedo marker
  they touch.
- **No flying through ships.** Craft cannot pass through an enemy ship's base to reach a target behind
  it. They may, though, go for a small-based ship sitting inside the footprint of a large base, if
  they can reach the small base. That is the only way attack craft may skip the first base they
  touch. Torpedoes of every kind can't do this.
- **A destroyed target.** A wave of bombers or assault boats that destroys a ship is used up and
  removed, even if some markers had not yet made their attacks.

### Fighters

Fighters are small, fast and agile, armed only to kill other ordnance.

- **Against ordnance:** remove both the fighter and the marker it meets. Fighters must always fight
  ordnance they touch, and craft that act as fighters while doing other jobs must too.
- They ignore friendly ordnance, but destroy any enemy torpedoes (and see boarding torpedoes, above).
- **Against ships:** nothing. Their guns can't hurt a ship, and they keep clear of its turrets. The
  marker stays in play.

#### Combat air patrol

Fighters touching a friendly ship may screen it against enemy ordnance. They are then on **combat air
patrol (CAP)**.

- Fighters may go on CAP at any point in their normal move, by moving onto a friendly ship. Fighters
  in a mixed wave must split off from the craft that aren't fighters first. Only fighters, and craft
  that act as fighters, may go on CAP.
- In the Manoeuvre Phase they move with their ship and stay touching it. They may **not** then move in
  the Ordnance Phase as well: no double moves. If going with the ship took them further than they
  could fly in one Ordnance Phase, they may not move in the other player's Ordnance Phase either,
  though they still guard the ship.
- They guard the ship against enemy ordnance it meets, even in the middle of its own move.
- Several fighters on CAP over one ship are separate markers, not a wave.
- **Blast Markers:** each CAP marker rolls on its own. If the ship they guard is destroyed, each marker
  rolls separately against the explosion; those that survive are ordinary markers again and may move
  in the next Ordnance Phase.
- In the game, each fighter on CAP stops one torpedo salvo or one attack craft squadron before the
  turrets fire, both markers being removed.
- CAP fighters don't attack torpedoes or mines launched by the ship they guard, or by friendly ships
  touching it (a combined salvo included). They do defend against enemy torpedoes and mines at every
  other time.
- Friendly attack craft may shelter inside the base of a ship that has fighters on CAP. Enemy fighters
  that intercept them there are not shot at by the ship's turrets.
- Craft that are not fighters cannot be used as CAP, so they can't ride along with a ship and strike
  an enemy it rams or touches in its move.
- **Craft that fight and do another job** (a fighter that can also bomb or board, for example) may only
  attack ships from CAP in the Ordnance Phase, unless an enemy ship moves into contact with them in its
  own Manoeuvre Phase, when they strike at once. They can't otherwise attack a ship until they leave
  CAP.
  - If your ship carries such craft on CAP while it rams or boards an enemy, they don't strike with it.
    They wait for the Ordnance Phase, and even then only attack if your ship ended its move touching
    the enemy, because craft that rode along in the Manoeuvre Phase can't move in the Ordnance Phase.
    If the two ships are locked in a boarding action, the craft attack in the Ordnance Phase, before
    the boarding is fought in the End Phase.
  - If an enemy ship rams or boards your ship while it has such craft on CAP, the enemy must follow the
    rules for moving into ordnance: after the ram (if it lands) the craft attack it at once and are
    removed. They also attack at once, ahead of the End Phase, if the enemy ends its move touching
    your ship to board it.
  - These CAP markers are not a wave: if one of them destroys the ship, no other marker is lost.
- **Leaving CAP.** Apart from being destroyed, fighters leave CAP only at the start of their own
  player's Manoeuvre Phase, or when their player moves ordnance in the other player's Ordnance Phase.
- A resilient craft on CAP (below) still moves with the ship even after it has used its save.

### Bombers

Bombers are slower, heavier craft carrying anti-ship weapons.

- **Against enemy fighters:** both markers are removed.
- **Against other ordnance:** the bombers get out of the way and nothing more happens. Both markers stay.
  This includes bombers that have a save.
- **Against a ship:**
  1. The ship's turrets fire at the wave first.
  2. Each bomber squadron left makes **D6 minus the ship's turrets** attacks (never fewer than 0).
  3. Each attack is a D6 against the ship's **lowest** Armour, ignoring shields. Each success does 1
     damage.
  4. Remove the bomber markers once they have attacked.
- Turrets **always** reduce a bomber's attacks, even when they chose to fire at torpedoes this phase and
  so could not shoot at the bombers.
- A hulk has no turrets, so nothing comes off the bombers' attacks.

> A wave of two bomber squadrons attacks a cruiser with two turrets. The cruiser rolls two dice to
> shoot at them. Each squadron left makes D6-2 attacks, and is then removed.

#### Turret suppression

Fighters flying with bombers draw the turrets' fire.

- Each fighter in a bomber wave that attacks a ship adds **+1 attack** to the wave's total, whether or
  not the turrets shoot it down.
- The bonus can never be more than the number of bombers left after the turrets have fired, and there
  must be at least one bomber left to get it.
- Turrets that hit the wave remove fighters before any other kind of marker.
- Fighters that never reach the ship, because enemy fighters (CAP included) stopped them, add nothing.

> A battleship sends a wave of three bombers and five fighters at a cruiser with three turrets and no
> CAP. The turrets roll 4, 5 and 6, and shoot down three fighters. Each of the three bombers makes
> D6-3 attacks. Only three of the five fighters can add their +1, as the wave has just three bombers:
> +3 attacks in all. Had the wave been four bombers and four fighters, the same three hits would take
> down three fighters, and all four fighters would add +1 for +4 attacks.

#### Torpedo bombers

Torpedo bombers are bombers carrying small anti-ship torpedoes, so they can strike from further off.

- They are launched like any other attack craft. Their speed is 20 cm, and they count as bombers when
  they meet other ordnance.
- At the start of any Ordnance Phase except the one in which they were launched, a torpedo bomber
  squadron may be replaced with a **Strength 2 torpedo salvo**. A whole wave may join its salvoes into
  one, as a squadron of ships does.
- The salvo then follows the torpedo rules, but it is low on fuel: it is removed at the end of the
  Ordnance Phase in which it was released. In the game it flies at once at the speed of its fleet's own
  torpedoes.
- Because they can't release in their launch phase, torpedo bombers that reach a target the turn they
  are launched get no attack. Use ordinary bombers for close work.

### Assault boats

Assault boats clamp onto an enemy hull and cut their way in, letting picked troops loose inside.

- **Against enemy fighters:** both markers are removed.
- **Against other ordnance:** the boats steer round it. Both markers stay.
- **Against a ship:** the turrets fire at them as at bombers. Each squadron left makes **one
  Hit-and-Run attack** (chapter 8) on the ship, there and then in the Ordnance Phase, and is removed.
- Assault boats move 30 cm in any direction each Ordnance Phase, unless the fleet list gives them
  another speed. The fleet lists say which ships carry them.

### Waves

A ship may launch its squadrons one by one, or join them into **waves**. Either way, every contact is
worked out marker by marker.

- A wave is formed only when its craft are launched. Place the markers touching one another (never
  stacked), and keep them together as they move.
- A wave is packed as tightly as possible: four in a square, six in two rows of three, and so on, never a long
  line.
- A wave of craft with different speeds moves at the speed of the slowest.
- A wave may split up during a move, but split squadrons can never join again.
- **Why fly in waves:** the target's turrets fire only once at the whole wave, so more of it gets
  through.
- **The cost:** any direct fire hit on a wave (batteries, lances or a sunburst cannon) destroys the
  **whole** wave. A wave that rolls a 6 for crossing Blast Markers is removed entire. And once a wave has
  attacked a ship it is gone, all of it, even if the ship dies before every marker has had its turn.
- When fighters or turrets attack a wave, the fighters in it are removed before anything else. Pack
  fighters into your bomber and assault boat waves to soak up the defence.

### Resilient attack craft

Some craft are **resilient**: they have a 4+ save against other ordnance.

- The save may be tried **once per Ordnance Phase**, whether the craft is attacking or attacked.
- A marker that saves stops where the contact happened, may not move further that phase, and has no save
  for the rest of the phase (or the rest of the Manoeuvre Phase, if it saved while on CAP). A marker that
  fails is removed at once.
- A second contact in the same phase removes the marker automatically, as if it were not resilient.
- A resilient marker that saves and finishes its move touching an enemy ship may attack it as normal. If
  it was stopped by fighters on CAP while already touching the ship, it may still finish its attack run
  on that ship, as long as it needs to move no further.
- Resilient craft that are fighters must always fight other ordnance they touch, even if they have
  another job as well.
- Resilient craft that are not fighters (resilient bombers, for example) pass by other ordnance except
  fighters, as other bombers and assault boats do.
- **The order.** The attacking player always decides the order in which contacts happen.

> Two resilient fighters meet two enemy fighters. Their player may send the first against one enemy,
> save, and then against the second: that second contact removes it automatically, while the other
> resilient fighter, untouched, flies on with its save unused. Or he may send one against each: if both
> save, both stay, but both stop where they met the enemy.

- **Two resilient markers** that attack each other and both save stay where they are, touching, until
  the next turn's Ordnance Phase. If either is attacked again in that phase, it is removed along with
  its attacker.
- **In a wave:** a marker that saves may not move again that phase, and splits off from the wave there.
  The rest of the wave, untouched, goes on with its move.

# 5. The Fire Phase

Ships attack in two ways. **Direct fire** (weapons batteries, lances and the sunburst cannon) strikes
almost at once, across tens of thousands of kilometres. **Ordnance** (torpedoes and attack craft) is
launched at the end of the Fire Phase and strikes later, in an Ordnance Phase (chapter 6). This
chapter covers direct fire, and the shields and Blast Markers that go with it.

## How the phase works

Fire with one ship at a time:

1. Pick a ship (or squadron, chapter 11).
2. Check which enemy ships are in range of its weapons.
3. Check which of its weapons have a target in their fire arc.
4. Resolve its fire.
5. Pick the next ship, until every ship you want to fire has fired.

At the end of the phase, launch any ordnance (chapter 6).

## Range and arc

- Measure from the firing ship's stem to the target's stem. A weapon whose range falls short may
  not fire at that target.
- A weapon may only fire at a target inside its fire arc. A ship whose target sits in its right arc
  may fire every weapon that covers the right arc at it.
- A ship may fire different weapons at different targets, each in its own arc.

## Choosing a target

**Fire at the nearest.** A close enemy is far more dangerous than a distant one, so a ship fires at
the **nearest enemy ship or squadron**. To fire at anything else, it must first pass a Resolve Test
on 2D6.

- Weapons that cannot reach the nearest enemy, being out of range or out of arc, may fire at
  another target without the test.
- A hulk (chapter 7) never forces the test by being nearest: a ship may fire past it freely. To
  fire *at* an enemy hulk that is not the nearest target, it does need the test.
- Firing at ordnance has its own rules (chapter 6).

**Splitting fire.** A ship may divide the firepower of its weapons batteries, or the Strength of its
lances, between several targets. It divides what is left after any halving for orders or damage,
and it still needs the Resolve Test for every target other than the nearest.

## Lances

A lance is a beam of enormous power that burns straight through armour.

- Roll one D6 per point of the lance's Strength.
- Each **4, 5 or 6** is a hit, **whatever the target's Armour**. Each hit does 1 damage.
- Blast Markers do not affect lances.

> A ship with lances of Strength 2 in the arc rolls 2D6 and scores a hit for each 4 or more.

## Weapons batteries

A weapons battery is rank upon rank of guns firing in a pattern of salvoes. How many of its shots
reach the target depends on the target's size and on which way it is heading, since a ship crossing
your sights (abeam) is much harder to hit than one coming at you or running away.

1. Take the battery's **firepower**.
2. Work out the target's type and **discipline** (chapter 1).
3. Find the firepower in the left-hand column of the gunnery table, and read across to the column
   for that target, after any column shifts (below). That is the number of dice you roll.
4. Each dice that equals or beats the target's **Armour on the side facing the firer** is a hit.
   Each hit does 1 damage.

When a ship fires several weapons that use the gunnery table, they fire together, but you work out
the dice for each kind of weapon on its own, and you choose the order in which they strike. A
squadron firing its batteries together suffers no column shift from Blast Markers its own ships
placed earlier in the same phase.

| 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

| 9 | 8 | 6 | 5 | 3 | 2 |

- Defences and ordnance have no discipline: every defence uses the Defences column and all ordnance the
  last column.
- A ship that moved less than 5 cm in its last move is shot at as a defence (chapter 4).
- **Firepower over 20** (usually a squadron firing together): read off 20, then read off the rest
  separately, and add. Two ships with 16 each make 32 in one broadside: the dice for 20 plus the
  dice for 12.
- A weapon whose rules say it always treats the target as closing still uses the Defences column
  against a defence, and still takes column shifts.

### Column shifts

Conditions shift you along the table. A shift **left** means more dice; a shift **right**, fewer.

| Condition | Shift |
|---|---|
| target within 15 cm | one column left |
| target more than 30 cm away | one column right |
| the line of fire passes through a Blast Marker, or the firer or the target has one touching its base | one column right |

- Shifts add together, but you can never go past the first or the last column.

> An enemy cruiser fires a battery of firepower 10 at a Freemen cruiser coming straight at it: a
> closing capital ship, 7 dice. Its front armour is 6+, so only sixes hit. Within 15 cm the
> column shifts left and it would roll 9 dice; beyond 30 cm it shifts right and it would roll 5.

## Shields

Nearly every ship is wrapped in energy shields that soak up the first hits of an attack.

- **Each shield stops one hit** from weapons batteries, lances or a sunburst cannon. Place a
  **Blast Marker** touching the ship's base for each hit a shield stops.
- Once the number of Blast Markers touching the base **equals the ship's shields**, the shields are
  down: every further hit damages the ship.
- In practice, when a ship is fired at, **its shields count one fewer for each Blast Marker
  touching its base**.
- The shields stay down until the ship moves away from those markers in a later Manoeuvre Phase.
- Shields give **no** protection against rams, bombers or torpedoes, which strike inside them, nor
  against ordnance generally.

> A ship with two shields takes three hits. The first two are stopped and two Blast Markers go
> against its base; the third does 1 damage. Every hit on it for the rest of the phase gets
> through, because its shields are down.

## Blast Markers

A Blast Marker stands for space torn up by battle: explosions, shock waves, radiation, wreckage and
unexploded warheads. The game uses round markers 2 cm across.

### When Blast Markers are placed

- One for each hit stopped by a ship's shields.
- When a ship is reduced to 0 hits, as its catastrophic damage result says (chapter 7).
- One for each sunburst cannon shot that misses.

### Where they go

- For hits on shields, the first marker goes on the line of fire from the firing ship, touching
  the target's base. Further markers are fanned out around it, touching the base.
- Markers are not stacked unless there is no room left around the base.
- Place them against the target, not against ships merely near it. As far as you can, keep them off
  the bases of other ships that are close by but not touching the target. A marker affects only what
  it physically touches; you can't pretend a ship isn't touching a marker that it is.
- If ships are touching the target, or crowded round a battleship whose markers fan out widely,
  markers may end up touching them too.
- A ship whose base overlaps the base of a ship with a marker touching it counts as touching that
  marker too. This goes one step only: a third ship overlapping the second, but not touching the
  marker, does not count.
- A ship that merely touches the base of a ship with a marker does not count as touching the marker.

### Blast Markers stay put

Once placed, a Blast Marker never moves. It becomes part of the battlefield until it is removed in an
End Phase (chapter 9). A marker touching a base affects the whole base, all round, in every arc.

### What they do

- **Manoeuvre.**
  - A ship that moves through any Blast Markers loses 5 cm of speed that phase, however many it
    crosses.
  - A ship that starts the Manoeuvre Phase touching Blast Markers counts as moving through them,
    even if it moves away from them.
  - A ship that cannot slow down (one on All Ahead Full, for example) and meets a Blast Marker in the
    last 5 cm of its move stops at the point where it touches the marker, and not before.
  - A ship with a shield value of 0 (it has none, or its shields have collapsed) that moves through
    any Blast Markers takes 1 damage on a D6 roll of 6. Roll once per move, however many it
    crosses. A ship whose shields are only down from taking fire does not roll.
- **Fire.** If the line of fire passes through a Blast Marker, or the firer or the target is touching
  one, batteries shift one column right. Lances and the sunburst cannon are not affected. Markers
  work the moment they are placed, so they can hinder later fire in the same phase.
- **Shields.** A ship fired at has one shield fewer for each Blast Marker touching it (above).
- **Command checks.** A ship touching a Blast Marker is under fire: -1 Resolve (chapter 4).
- **Ordnance** that crosses Blast Markers may be destroyed (chapter 6).

## The sunburst cannon

The sunburst cannon hurls a single shell at nearly the speed of light, which bursts at a set
distance with more force than a dozen plasma bombs. It is carried in the prow, where the drive can
absorb the recoil.

**The template** is a circle 5 cm across, with a hole 1.2 cm across at its centre.

1. **Aim.** Place the template anywhere in the ship's front arc with its edge between **30 and 150
   cm** from the firing ship. It need not be centred on a ship, and may touch several.
2. **Brace.** Before any dice are rolled, your opponent decides whether to brace each ship or
   squadron the shell might hit, including ships it could reach by scattering.
3. **Scatter.** Roll the scatter dice.
   - A **hit** on the scatter dice: the template stays where it is.
   - An arrow: the template moves that way by the distance rolled: **1D6 cm** if it was placed
     within 45 cm, **2D6 cm** if it was placed 45 to 60 cm away, **3D6 cm** if beyond 60 cm.
4. **Damage.**
   - Every ship touched by the template takes 1 hit.
   - A ship touched by the centre hole takes **D6 hits** instead, whatever its Armour.
   - Shields stop these hits in the normal way.
   - All ordnance touched by the template is removed.
5. **A miss.** If the template touches nothing once it has moved, put a single Blast Marker in its
   place.

**Limits.**

- It cannot fire through anything that blocks a line of fire, such as planets, moons and asteroid
  fields. It may be aimed at one of them: if the scatter dice shows a hit, place D6 Blast Markers
  touching that feature's edge.
- Lock On and Reload Ordnance do nothing for it.
- It may not fire while the ship is crippled, or while it is on any of these orders: Burn Retros,
  All Ahead Full, Brace for Impact! or Come to New Heading.

## Weapons that strike all round

A few weapons, and some events such as exploding ships, do not aim at a target: they strike everything
around them. Such an effect is not blocked by anything that blocks a line of fire (hulks, minefields
or celestial phenomena), and protections that a fleet's rules describe as saving against aimed fire
do not save against it. Shields still work against it as normal.

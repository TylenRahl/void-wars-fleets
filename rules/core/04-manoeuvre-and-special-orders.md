# 4. The Manoeuvre Phase and Special Orders

In the Manoeuvre Phase you bring your ships into position: closing on a target, crossing its bow,
hiding behind a planet or running from a ship you cannot beat. A captain can also push his ship
past its normal limits by putting it on a **special order**.

## Moving a ship

Move your ships one at a time:

1. Pick a ship (or squadron, chapter 11).
2. If you want it on a special order, declare the order and take its Command check (below).
3. Move it.
4. Pick the next, until every ship you want to move has moved.

Ships never move at the same time, not even ships of one squadron: move each in turn, in any order
you like.

### Distance

- A ship may move **up to its speed**, in a straight line straight ahead, with turns as allowed
  below.
- **Minimum move: half its speed.** A ship carries huge momentum, and slowing it without
  preparation would tear the hull apart, so it must move at least half its speed every Manoeuvre
  Phase. Only Burn Retros lets it go slower or hold still.
- If damage, Blast Markers or anything else leave a ship unable to make half its speed, it must
  move as far as it can instead.
- In the game, a ship you leave unmoved when you end the phase goes straight ahead by its minimum
  move (half its speed, or the full distance on All Ahead Full).

### Barely moving: counts as a defence

A ship that moved **less than 5 cm** in its last move is shot at as a **defence** on the gunnery
table, the easiest target there is (chapter 5). This includes a ship holding still on Burn Retros
or a ship forced to stand still. In the game it applies from the ship's first move; before the
battle's first move nobody counts as a defence for this.

### Turning

A ship travels straight ahead, and may make **one turn** during its move unless its orders say
otherwise. The turn may be up to the angle in its profile, 45° or 90°.

Big ships take time to answer the helm. Before turning, a ship must have moved this far in the
current Manoeuvre Phase (movement in earlier phases does not count):

| Ship | Distance before it may turn |
|---|---|
| battleship | 15 cm |
| cruiser | 10 cm |
| escort | none: it may turn at any point in its move |

A planet's gravity well can give a ship free turns as well (chapter 10).

### Losing speed

| Cause | Speed lost |
|---|---|
| the ship moves through one or more Blast Markers | 5 cm that phase, however many it crosses (chapter 5) |
| the ship is crippled | 5 cm (chapter 7) |
| the ship has one or more Thrusters Damaged criticals | 10 cm, counted once however many it has (chapter 7) |

## Special orders

There are six special orders. Each makes a ship better at one thing for the turn, usually at the
cost of something else. A ship or squadron can be on only one special order at a time.

### Command checks

To put a ship on special orders, declare the order before it moves, then take a **Command check**:
a Resolve Test on 2D6.

- Equal to or under its Resolve: the ship is on the order. Mark it with an order marker and move
  it as the order allows.
- Over its Resolve: it fails and stays on no order.
- **One failure ends it.** Once one Command check fails, none of your ships may take another
  Command check this turn. The moment has passed in the confusion. The one exception is Brace for
  Impact!, which may still be tried.
- So you may keep putting ships on orders, one after another, until every ship has one or one
  check fails.
- Any check a rule lets you take for free (no roll needed) must be used before a check fails.
- All orders except Brace for Impact! are checked in the Manoeuvre Phase, before the ship moves.

Two modifiers apply to Command checks, and to no other Resolve Test:

| Situation | Modifier to Resolve |
|---|---|
| **Under fire:** a Blast Marker is touching the ship's base | -1 |
| **Enemy contacts:** any enemy ship is on special orders (its energy makes it easy to read) | +1 |

Resolve can never go above 10, and a roll of 11 or 12 always fails.

> The Freemen cruiser *Vigilant* (Resolve 7) wants to go All Ahead Full to run down an enemy
> cruiser that went on Lock On last turn. The enemy's order gives the *Vigilant* +1, so it tests
> against 8. It rolls a 2 and a 6, for 8: just enough.

### The six orders

"Half" always rounds up. Half effect on weapons means halving the firepower of weapons batteries
and the Strength of lances; ordnance means torpedoes and launch bays.

| Order | Speed | Turns | Batteries and lances | Ordnance | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **All Ahead Full** | speed + 4D6 cm, and the ship must move the full distance | none | half; no sunburst cannon | full | the extra speed. A squadron rolls the 4D6 once for all its ships. Needed to ram (chapter 8). |
| **Come to New Heading** | half to full speed | up to two | half; no sunburst cannon | full | a second turn. The second turn follows every rule for the first: a cruiser that went 10 cm before its first turn must go 10 cm more before its second. |
| **Burn Retros** | zero to half speed | up to one | half; no sunburst cannon | full | the ship may make its one turn without moving forward first, and may hold its position |
| **Lock On** | half to full speed | none | full | full | in the Fire Phase, re-roll any dice that missed for lances and weapons batteries. The ship holds a steady course, so it may not turn. |
| **Reload Ordnance** | half to full speed | up to one | full | full | all the ship's ordnance is reloaded (chapter 6) |
| **Brace for Impact!** | half to full speed | up to one | half; no sunburst cannon | half | a 4+ save against each point of damage (below) |

The sunburst cannon is not helped by Lock On or Reload Ordnance (chapter 5).

### Brace for Impact!

The captain sounds the alarm: power goes to the shields, blast doors slam shut and the crew holds
on. Brace for Impact! works differently from the other orders.

- **When.** A ship may try to brace at **any** moment it is about to take damage, in either
  player's turn: against gunfire, ordnance, a ram (given or taken), an asteroid field, or a
  Hit-and-Run attack.
- **Before the dice.** Decide before your opponent rolls anything for the attack, including any
  roll that sets how strong a weapon is. Against ordnance, decide before the turrets are rolled.
  Brace only counts against attacks whose hit dice are rolled after it is declared: you cannot
  wait to see how good a salvo or a sunburst shot is.
- **The save.** While braced, the ship rolls a D6 for each point of damage to the ship itself;
  each 4+ saves that point. Hits stopped by shields (or by any other protection that stops hits
  first) are not damage, so they get no save.
- **What it does not stop.** No save against critical damage caused by hits that got through
  normally, and none against anything that happens in a boarding action, critical damage
  included. It **does** save against the critical damage of a Hit-and-Run attack.
- **How long.** The ship stays braced until the end of its next turn. Bracing replaces whatever
  order the ship was on, but what that order already did stands: a ship that reloaded stays
  reloaded, and a ship on All Ahead Full must still finish its move at full speed.
- **The cost.** While braced the ship's batteries and lances are halved, its ordnance is halved
  and it may not fire a sunburst cannon. Its shields and turrets are not affected. A braced ship
  that is also crippled halves again: its weapons and ordnance are halved twice (rounding up each
  time). And it may take **no** special order at all in its next turn.
- **A failed brace.** A ship that fails the check gets no second try against the attacker that
  prompted it: it must wait until that ship, squadron, wave of ordnance or event has finished. It
  may try again before the next attacker.
- A squadron braces as one: every ship in it is braced, not only the one under fire (chapter 11).
